# Verification-First Website Cleanup: A Public Example

A common small-business website problem is not a dramatic technical failure. It is unfinished material that stays public long after the site launches.

Examples include:

- “Lorem Ipsum”
- “Add your title here”
- “Project name here”
- sample phone numbers or email addresses
- website-builder instructions
- old business names or unrelated contact information
- duplicate/staging pages that remain indexed

These defects are easy for an owner to stop noticing because the owner already knows what the business is supposed to say. A new customer does not.

## The verification-first method

### Before

Record the exact public page and the exact visible defect.

Do not rely on an old screenshot or a task saying it was fixed.

### During

Correct only the authorized content or configuration. Avoid turning a bounded cleanup into an unnecessary redesign.

### After

Read the public page again from the customer-facing surface.

Check:

- the placeholder is gone
- the correct replacement is live
- the CTA still works
- no new duplicate or formatting issue was introduced
- mobile/customer paths still make sense

### Evidence

Record what changed and where it can be independently seen.

That final read-back is the difference between an automation that *ran* and work that is actually *complete*.

> **Automation is not completion. Verification is completion.**
